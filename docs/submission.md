## Project summary

OneStop brings the research behind your next move into one place. Rental listings are scattered across sites like Zillow and Apartments.com, and the details that matter are often buried. We organize housing research around where you actually need to be—your workplace, school, or another daily destination—and compare rent share, utility inclusion, walking time, bus routes, and listing features. A great home doesn't disappear because it misses one filter: OneStop keeps near-matches visible and shows the exact compromise needed to unlock them. The result is a shortlist you can explain: what fits your life, what you're sacrificing, and what you still need to verify.

## Track justification

Housing search is an optimization problem with competing constraints: cost, commute, layout, utilities, and personal priorities. OneStop evaluates these together, preserves near-matches, and shows how a small requirement change alters the available options. Our CMU demo makes this concrete: increasing a $1,200 personal rent cap by $7.50 brings a researched 2-bedroom, 2-bathroom floor plan within the rent and walking requirements. ## AI used to build

We used Codex to plan, implement, debug, and test the application; Claude Code for independent design review and research experiments; and OpenCode Go for additional implementation review. We iterated on the rendered interface and tested the complete housing-search workflow, including source evidence, commute calculations, utility uncertainty, comparisons, and saved shortlists.

## AI integrated into the application

Claude-powered web research discovers additional rental leads across public housing sources and organizes them into structured results. Source adapters validate and normalize supported listings, while explicit rules calculate rent shares, evaluate requirements, and identify useful alternatives. We keep source links and missing information visible so an AI-generated lead is never presented as a verified home.
